The heart of the monument

The Taj Mahal mausoleum is a masterpiece of Mughal architecture, built of white marble.

The building houses, at its center, the cenotaphs of Mumtaz Mahal and Shah Jahan. The quality of the materials, particularly the stone inlaid using the pietra dura technique, reflects the exceptional craftsmanship of the era.

Spaces for Contemplation

The indoor and outdoor spaces have been designed to offer a variety of atmospheres.

Inside, the skillful use of marble and inlays creates areas of light and shadow that highlight the cenotaphs. The surrounding areas feature a traditional layout enhanced by the presence of minarets.

Floor plan of the Taj Mahal

Spatial Organization

The entire site is designed according to the principles of symmetry typical of the Mughal Empire.

The monument's layout integrates the various elements—minarets, domes, and circulation spaces—to naturally guide visitors through the site. The result is a harmonious layout that allows visitors to understand the structure while discovering its subtleties.

A Harmony of Materials

Every material used contributes to the Taj Mahal’s uniqueness. The marble, chosen for its purity, blends perfectly with the stones.

The use of red sandstone and semi-precious stones (pietra dura) creates a striking contrast. The restoration work reflects a commitment to preserving the authenticity and durability of the original materials.
