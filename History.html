The Builder of the Taj Mahal

1526 – Foundation of the Mughal Empire

Babur, a descendant of Genghis Khan and Tamerlane, founds the Mughal Empire in India following his victory at the Battle of Panipat.

1612 – Marriage of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal

Shah Jahan marries Arjumand Banu Begum, known as Mumtaz Mahal, who becomes his favorite and the inspiration for the future Taj Mahal.

1628 – Shah Jahan ascends to the throne

Shah Jahan becomes Mughal Emperor, and his reign is marked by a cultural and architectural golden age, notably the construction of the Taj Mahal.

1631 – Death of Mumtaz Mahal

Mumtaz Mahal died while giving birth to her 14th child. Deeply grieved, Shah Jahan decided to build the Taj Mahal in her memory.

1632 – Construction of the Taj Mahal begins

Work on the mausoleum begins, mobilizing more than 20,000 artisans from India, Persia, the Ottoman Empire, and Europe.

1648 – Completion of the main mausoleum

The main mausoleum of the Taj Mahal is completed, while the annex buildings and gardens are finished five years later, in 1653.

1666 – Death of Shah Jahan

Shah Jahan dies in captivity and is buried alongside Mumtaz Mahal in the Taj Mahal.

1857 – End of the Mughal Empire

The last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah II, is exiled by the British following a failed revolt, marking the official end of the Mughal Empire.

1908 – Restoration of the Taj Mahal

Lord Curzon, the British viceroy, ordered a major restoration of the Taj Mahal to preserve its splendor after years of neglect.

1983 – Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Taj Mahal is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, highlighting its global cultural and architectural significance.
